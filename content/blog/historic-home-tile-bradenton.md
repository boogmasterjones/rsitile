A lot of our downtown Bradenton work happens in homes that were built long before today's tile sizes, adhesives, or waterproofing methods existed. That's not a problem — it just means the approach is different from a straightforward remodel in newer construction. This guide covers what to actually expect.

## Tile & Grout Matching in an Older Home

The single most common question we get on a historic Bradenton renovation is whether we can match what's already there. Sometimes we can get close — certain classic tile sizes and grout colors are still made. More often, the exact original has been discontinued for years, especially in homes built mid-century or earlier.

When an exact match isn't realistic, we'll lay out the honest options: a close match that reads as original from a normal viewing distance, or a deliberate transition — a threshold, a change in pattern, or a complementary tile — that looks intentional rather than like a patch job.

## Why Older Subfloors Need Extra Prep

Decades of settling, humidity, and prior renovations can leave a subfloor uneven in ways that don't show up until the old flooring comes out. It's also common to find multiple layers of old adhesive, underlayment, or even a previous tile job underneath what's currently down. Skipping proper leveling here is one of the most common causes of cracked tile or grout lines a year or two after installation — it's worth the extra prep time, especially in a home with real age on it.

## Match the Original, or Modernize?

There's no single right answer here — it comes down to what you want the finished space to feel like. Some homeowners want every room to read as consistent with the house's original character, using classic tile sizes, patterns, and grout colors throughout. Others treat each room's remodel as a chance to modernize that specific space, especially a bathroom or kitchen that's due for a full update anyway. We can walk you through what will look intentional either way, based on the home's age, style, and the rest of what's original.

## Material Choices That Respect the Home

Large-format modern tile can sometimes look out of place against a historic home's smaller original openings and trim details. For homeowners who want a period-appropriate feel, classic subway tile, smaller-format hex or mosaic patterns, and traditional grout lines tend to look more at home than oversized modern porcelain slabs — though the right call always depends on the specific house and what you're going for.
