Lots of our downtown Bradenton work is in old homes. Many were built long before today's tile sizes existed. The same goes for today's glues and ways to seal out water. That's not a problem. It just means our work differs from a simple remodel in a new home. This guide tells you what to really expect.

## Tile & Grout Matching in an Older Home

Here is the most common question we get on a historic Bradenton remodel. Can we match what is already there? Sometimes we can get close. Some classic tile sizes and grout colors are still made. But more often, the exact old tile hasn't been made for years. That is extra true in homes built in the 1950s or before.

When an exact match can't be found, we'll lay out your honest choices. One is a close match. It looks like the first tile from a normal distance. Another is a planned switch to new tile. That could be a doorway strip or a new pattern. Or it could be a tile that goes well with the old one. It looks like it was done on purpose. It won't look like a patch job.

## Why Older Floors Need Extra Prep

Decades of settling, damp air, and past remodels can leave a floor uneven. Often you can't see it until the old floor comes out. We also often find layers of old glue or old base under the floor. Sometimes there is even an old tile job hiding under there. Skipping good leveling here is a top cause of cracked tile. It also causes cracked grout lines a year or two after the job. So the extra prep time is worth it. That is extra true in a home with real age on it.

## Match the Old Look, or Make It Modern?

There's no one right answer here. It comes down to how you want the finished space to feel. Some people want each room to match the house's first look. They use classic tile sizes, patterns, and grout colors all through the house. Others use each room's remodel as a chance to make that space modern. That is common for a bathroom or kitchen due for a full update. We can help you plan a look that feels on purpose either way. We'll go by the home's age and style. We'll also look at what else in the home is still from the start.

## Tile Choices That Fit the Home

Big modern tile can look out of place in a historic home. It can clash with small old doorways and trim. Do you want a look that fits the home's time? Then classic subway tile tends to look more at home. So do small hex or mosaic patterns and old-style grout lines. They often fit better than big modern porcelain slabs. But the right call always depends on the house. It also depends on the look you want.
