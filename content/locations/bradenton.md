        <h2>Bradenton Neighborhoods We Serve</h2>
        <p>Bradenton isn't one uniform market — a home in the historic downtown grid needs a different approach than a new build near Lakewood Ranch. Here's how that plays out across the areas we work in most:</p>
        <h3>Downtown Bradenton &amp; the Riverwalk</h3>
        <p>Older homes near downtown and the Manatee River Riverwalk often have original tile or grout that's decades old, uneven subfloors, and layouts that don't match anything sold today. Careful matching, transition planning, and substrate leveling matter more here than in newer construction.</p>

        <p>Homes near Palma Sola Bay deal with the same salt-air, moisture-heavy conditions we see on the coast in Sarasota and Venice — porcelain and stain-resistant grout are usually the better call over standard ceramic.</p>
        <h3>Cortez</h3>
        <p>Cortez's historic fishing village sits right on the water, and its mix of older cottages and newer waterfront rebuilds means we're regularly balancing tile-matching work with full moisture-tolerant installs a few doors down from each other.</p>
        <h3>Lakewood Ranch Area</h3>
        <p>East Bradenton's steady growth toward Lakewood Ranch keeps us busy with whole-home tile in new construction — kitchens, floors, and bathrooms installed as part of a fresh build rather than a remodel.</p>
        <h3>Near Anna Maria Island</h3>
        <p>Mainland Bradenton homes close to the Anna Maria Island causeway see some of the same coastal exposure as the barrier island itself, which is another spot where material choice matters more than it would further inland.</p>
